## 7. This / That / These / Those

A major new target is:
- **this**
- **that**
- **these**
- **those**

The PowerPoint contains answer patterns including:
- Those are
- These are
- That is
- This is

It explicitly says:

**Demonstrate — Use: this / that / these / those**

### Recommended concept
Teach two contrasts:

#### Distance
- this / these = near
- that / those = farther away

#### Number
- this / that = one thing
- these / those = more than one thing

### Suggested board layout

| | Near | Far |
|---|---|---|
| One | this | that |
| More than one | these | those |

### Adult-appropriate physical demonstration
Use real classroom objects:
- Hold one pen: **This is a pen.**
- Point to one book across the room: **That is a book.**
- Hold two markers: **These are markers.**
- Point to two chairs across the room: **Those are chairs.**

## 13. Suggested 105-Minute Lesson Structure

This is a suggested structure, not a requirement of the PowerPoint.

### 6:30–6:40 — Warm-up / review
Use real classroom objects to review:
- in
- on
- under

Examples:
- Put a pen on a notebook.
- Put the pen under the notebook.
- Put the pen in a bag.

Ask:
- Where is the pen?
- Is it under the notebook?

### 6:40–6:55 — Sentence building and punctuation
Use WB p.26-type material.

Focus on:
- sentence order
- capital letter
- period vs. question mark

Have students reconstruct short sentences before writing.

### 6:55–7:10 — Location speaking practice
Use real objects.

Partner A gives an instruction:
- Put the pencil under the book.
- Put the marker on the table.
- Put the notebook in the bag.

Partner B performs the action.

Then switch roles.

### 7:10–7:25 — This / That / These / Those
Teach physically:
- This is a pen.
- That is a clock.
- These are markers.
- Those are chairs.

Then students practice with real objects in the classroom.

### 7:25–7:40 — Partner identification task
One student points to an object or objects.

The partner produces a sentence:
- This is a notebook.
- That is a computer.
- These are pencils.
- Those are books.

Then add location:
- Those books are on the table.

### 7:40–7:50 — Paper activity
Students complete the corresponding worksheet section after oral practice.

### 7:50–8:00 — Break
If the class normally has a break, this is a reasonable point.

### 8:00–8:10 — Three speaking stations

#### Station 1 — Location
Practice:
- Where is ...?
- Where are ...?
- in / on / under

#### Station 2 — Demonstratives
Practice:
- this
- that
- these
- those

Use real objects.

#### Station 3 — Questions / quantities
Practice:
- Is it ...?
- Are they ...?
- How many ...?
- There are ...

### 8:10–8:15 — Exit check
Ask individual students 1–2 short questions:
- Where is the stapler?
- Is the notebook on the desk?
- What is this?
- What are those?
- How many rulers are there?

The goal is comprehension and usable English, not perfect grammar.

## 14. How to Use the Three Adults

Avoid having all three adults circulate randomly during every activity.

### Teacher
- introduces/models language
- controls whole-class pacing

### Assistant 1
- runs a speaking station
- monitors singular/plural patterns
- models rather than over-explains

### Assistant 2
- runs another speaking station
- gives extra task support
- checks the Farsi-speaking student after whole-class directions

One adult should briefly verify that the Farsi-speaking student understands each new task.

Useful check:
- What do I do?
- Who do I work with?
- Do I speak, write, point, or listen?
- When do we switch?

## 19. Teaching the Farsi-Speaking Student

A major difficulty may be understanding **what the class is doing**, not just vocabulary.

Therefore:
- Give whole-class directions in simple English.
- Model the first example.
- Walk briefly to the student.
- Point to the relevant Farsi task cue.
- Confirm the student starts correctly.
- Return to whole-class support.

Avoid turning every activity into private translation.

Use Farsi as a bridge back into English.

---

## 20. Error Correction

During speaking tasks, prioritize:
- is / are
- it / they
- this / that / these / those
- in / on / under
- question vs. statement
- singular vs. plural

Lower priority during fluency:
- missing articles
- minor pronunciation issues
- small errors that do not block meaning

Prefer recasting:

Student:
> Books is under table.

Teacher:
> Yes — the books **are under the table**.

Have the student repeat once, then continue.

## 21. Adult-Appropriate Interaction Principles

Avoid framing activities as children's games.

Prefer:
- partner practice
- speaking task
- communication challenge
- information gap
- accuracy check
- role-play
- station practice
- comprehension check

Real classroom objects are appropriate for adults because they make beginner language concrete and reduce translation demands.

---

## 22. Key Lesson Success Criteria

By the end of class, students should ideally be able to:
- identify classroom objects
- understand in / on / under
- describe where an object is
- ask Where is ...? / Where are ...?
- answer with It is ... / They are ...
- distinguish this / that / these / those
- use singular and plural forms more accurately
- identify whether a sentence needs a period or question mark
- produce a short sentence with correct initial capitalization
- understand simple quantity language such as There are 12 rulers ...

Prioritize usable comprehension and speaking over perfect mastery of every workbook item.
